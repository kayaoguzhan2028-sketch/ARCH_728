Ankara Clay

Ankara clay is not merely a passive ground layer on which constructions are built in the context of urban development; it is a dynamic actor that directly shapes the city's urban landscape, hydrological balance, ecological history, and vernacular construction culture.

Geotechnical Characteristics and Soil Behavior

Ankara clay is classified as a "challenging" soil profile in engineering and building sciences literature. The fundamental characteristics that determine its soil behavior are:

Overconsolidated Structure: Due to the heavy sedimentary loads it was exposed to during geological times, it exhibits a highly tight, stiff consistency and a high sensitivity to disturbance.

High Plasticity and Montmorillonite Content: The high percentage of montmorillonite in the clay's mineralogical structure causes significant volumetric swelling when it absorbs water, and the formation of deep shrinkage cracks due to water loss during dry periods.

Low Shear Strength and Landslide Risk: Clayey slopes that reach water saturation during seasons with excessive precipitation lose their shear strength, becoming risky areas for local landslides and earth slips.

Spatial Distribution and Geological Formations

Ankara clay consists of lacustrine and fluvial deposits accumulated during different geological periods (Miocene, Pliocene, Oligocene) in various basins of the city.

Region / AxisRelevant Formation and Lithological Content
Center and Southern Axis (Çankaya, Balgat, ODTÜ, Beytepe, Gölbaşı) Gölbaşı Formation (Pliocene): Also known as the "Red Formation"; lacustrine and alluvial deposits where calcite and clay cement bind conglomerate, sandstone, and mudstone.
Western and Southwestern Axis (Etimesgut, Sincan, Temelli) Karakoçaş Formation (Upper Miocene): Bentonitic clay levels located within units consisting of red, gray, and mottled coarse conglomerate and sandstone.
Northern and Eastern Peripheries (Hatip Ovası, Mamak, Altındağ) Pliocene aged clayey series (structures not containing groundwater) and clay-rich slope rubbles located on metamorphic/volcanic units. Also, claystone alternations in the Oligocene aged Ağaçali Member.

Urban Landscape and Architectural Effects

The physical nature of clay shapes architectural design and urban infrastructure both as a threatening factor and a cultural resource:

Soil-Structure Interaction (Settlement and Cracks): Swelling and shrinkage cycles cause periodic differential settlements and structural/cladding cracks, especially in shallow-founded structures or buildings with poor soil isolation.

Hydrological Resistance (Impermeability): The fine-grained and tight texture of the clay limits the infiltration of rainwater underground. While this situation causes groundwater poverty in regions like the Hatip Plain, it increases urban surface runoff (flood risk) during sudden precipitation.

Vernacular Material Source: Ankara clay is historically the foundation stone of the city's construction memory. The Çubuk and Gölbaşı basins have hosted the brick and roof tile clay deposits used in the city's historical masonry structures. Kızılcahamam and Bala have provided raw materials for the bentonite and ceramic clay industry.
