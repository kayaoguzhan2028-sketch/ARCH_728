The Ground Speaks Animation Field Report
ARCH 728 · Spring 2026

Project 2026-9

Project Title

Memory Beneath the Concrete: Fluid Ground, Lost Streams, and the Resistance of Invasive Spaces in Ankara

Author

Rabia ÇELİK

Research Question

How does the ground within Ankara's urban soil metabolism—sealed by political and administrative decisions—reveal its ecological presence and lost memory as a spatial 'silent rebellion' through floods, swelling clay, and invasive plants in residual spaces?

Position Statement

In Ankara, the urban ground is a commodified hybrid structure rendered invisible by white spots on planning documents, or encoded during construction processes merely as a static substrate and an 'excavation load' to be transported. However, the soil refuses to be a passive material beneath the concrete layers cast over it by administrative decisions; it carries out a physical and metabolic 'silent rebellion' through the swelling of clay layers, the floods of culverted streams, and the invasive plants resisting within the city's pores. By making visible these dynamic acts of resistance by Ankara's soil within the urban infrastructure, this study advocates for making urban design more inclusive, encompassing both nature and the soil's own processes.

Integrated Typologies & Spatial Dynamics

  • The Swelling Muscle (Ankara Clay): Highly overconsolidated and montmorillonite-rich, this clay actively interacts with the urban foundations, causing periodic differential settlements and serving as an impermeable barrier that exacerbates urban surface runoff.
  • Entombed Waters (Lost Streams): The historical ecological corridors of Bentderesi, İncesu, and Kavaklıdere have been forced into concrete culverts. They represent a concealed hydrological network that reclaims its presence through urban flooding when rigid infrastructure fails.
  • Anthropogenic Excavations & Voids: The displacement of soil has created massive artificial topographies (Excavation Mountains) in Mamak and İncek, while abandoned foundation pits in Çukurambar and Susuz have evolved into deep artificial ponds. These voids act as biodiversity hotspots where ruderal species aggressively reclaim the terrain.
  • Invasive Plants: Abandoned soils, excavation pits, concrete borders and disrupted natural areas are open to invasive plants. It is a way of nature reminding itself and showing its power how it can manage any situation and survive in a challenging context.

Proposed Method

2D Vector and Collage-Based Cyclic Animation (Seamless GIF Loop): Ankara's sterile, technical development plan, composed of gray and white tones, will be framed as a "digital operating table" (base map). On this static map, the suppressed metabolic flows of the soil will be gradually set in motion, turning it into a dynamic GIF animation that forms a seamless loop.

Biomorphic Deformation and Layering (Visceral Animation): Hygienic and geometric urban lines will be animated using anatomical deformation techniques (Contraction and Swelling of Clay, Rupturing and Bleeding of Streams, Infection and Overflow of Excavation Hills, Spiral Network Propagation of Invasive Plants) to render visible the soil's responses to administrative sealing and physical pressures.
