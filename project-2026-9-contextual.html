The Ground Speaks

For too long, the modern city has treated the earth beneath it as a sterile slate—a dead, abstract geometry waiting to be paved, piped, and partitioned. In Ankara, the vibrant geological and hydrological memory of the steppe has been entombed beneath asphalt and concrete. We have exiled our soil to the peripheries to build artificial mountains of excavation waste, while burying our life-giving streams in subterranean culverts. We have attempted to silence the deeply overconsolidated, swelling clay, ignoring the metabolic realities of the ground in favor of an extractive, accelerated urbanism. The land has been stripped of its ecological agency, reduced to mere 'real estate' and 'excavation load' in the sterilized ledgers of administrative planning.

But the ground refuses to be passive; it is engaged in a visceral, silent rebellion. The sealed soil retaliates with floods when it can no longer breathe; the trapped clay swells and fractures our rigid foundations; and the forgotten voids become sanctuaries where invasive roots and spontaneous flora fiercely reclaim the concrete ruins. This is an awakening to the 'hybrid ground'—a call to cease our war of suppression against the earth. We must fundamentally redesign our spatial practices to embrace, rather than pave over, these metabolic fluxes. By acknowledging the living, moving, and bleeding infrastructure beneath our feet, we can forge an inclusive urbanism that honors the untamed memory of the soil.
