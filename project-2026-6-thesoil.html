Once stripped of its rich, alluvial top layer by brick factories, the soil is now being forced into an ongoing, toxic transformation. What was once a vital ecological resource has become a chaotic repository for foreign components: fragments of brick, concrete, ceramic, steel, metal, plastic, glass, wood and animal bone. This continuous influx of synthetic and organic debris deeply disrupts the soil's natural structure. Stripped of its metabolic richness, the soil is trapped in a loop of constant degradation.

Once characterized by its rich clay, silt, and natural organic matter, the soil is currently trapped in an ongoing, toxic transformation. What began as a historical extraction of its fertile layers has mutated into a continuous cycle of contamination, deeply fracturing the soil's natural stratification and transforming a living ecosystem into a synthetic residue.

These interventions change the soil's components. A mix of brick fragments, steel, and other industrial wastes overwhelms the natural minerals, making nutrients unreachable for plants. At the same time, this waste crushes the soil and cuts off oxygen, causing microorganisms to suffocate and rot. Without air, the water trapped within these layers stops supporting life and absorbs heavy metals leaking from plastics and industrial waste. Therefore, degradation of the soil is a continuous process. Driven by these intersecting chemical reactions, the soil is actively losing its vital biochemical cycling capabilities and richness, progressively transforming from a dynamic, living ecosystem into a residue.
