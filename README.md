## About

This is the public digital atlas for *ARCH 728: Reflections on Landscape*, a graduate-level design research studio at the Middle East Technical University (METU), Department of Architecture.

The studio investigates soil as a metabolically active urban medium — continuously produced, displaced, transformed, and regulated through intertwined human and non-human forces. Centered on Ankara, the research examines the ground not as a passive substrate but as a dynamic force shaping the city's spatial and political transformations.

This site is developed as the course's dedicated web presence, exploring digital representation as a medium for architectural theory. It functions as a static platform built for architectural presentation and spatial communication — serving both as a public record of student research and as an experiment in how design knowledge can be structured, layered, and made navigable on the web.

---

## Purpose

Academic knowledge is too often confined to institutional walls — theses, jury rooms, and printed proceedings that rarely reach the communities they concern. This atlas is built on the conviction that design research should be accessible, legible, and open.

By making student work publicly available as an interactive digital platform, this project participates in a broader effort to democratize academic knowledge: to bring rigorous spatial thinking out of the university and into dialogue with the city, its residents, and the wider public.

The site functions as a living archive — not a final product, but an ongoing record of how a group of researchers engaged with a city's ground.

## Structure

- **Manifesto (Homepage)** — `index.html`, the studio's framing statement and entry point
- **Atlas (Explore)** — `explore.html`, an interactive map locating each research project within Ankara, built on OpenStreetMap via Leaflet.js, with a by-map and by-content view
- **Project Pages** — Individual documentation for each of the 8 studio projects (Spring 2026 cycle), including field reports, drawings, and spatial analyses
- **Year Navigation** — The atlas is organized by academic year (`2026-pages/`), structured to grow with each new studio cycle

## Spring 2026 Projects

Eight student research projects, each investigating a distinct site or condition of soil transformation around Ankara:

1. **Çayırhan Ash Lake** — *Leakage*: how ash and slag from the Çayırhan Thermal Power Plant seep into soil, water, and bird migration routes.
2. **Hıdırlık Tepe** — *What the Ground Carries?*: how the ground of Hıdırlık Hill has been reshaped through extraction, settlement, and demolition.
3. **Sifting Ankara Soil** — a forensic, multi-sensory inventory of soil errors and sediment scenes across Ankara.
4. **Bilkent Territory** — mapping the soil excavated for Bilkent City Hospital and its surrounding sections, 2007–2023.
5. **An Idle Structure Between Soil and City** — the effects of the Grand Çankaya Hotel's ~30-year abandonment on the soil around it.
6. **Imrahor Valley** — soil, factories, and the storyline of Ankara's Imrahor Valley.
7. **Migrant Ground / The Figures of Migrant Soil** — five ontological stages of ground in motion (nomadic, barbaric, vagabond, proletarianized, civilized) as soil moves through Ankara's excavation apparatus, read through Thomas Nail's kinopolitics.
8. **Metabolizing the Construct** — the Çubuk 1 Dam and the helical metabolism of its landscape.

Each project page includes research questions, position statement, methodology, and field data specific to its site.

## Quick Facts

**What is ARCH 728?**
A graduate-level design research studio (course code ARCH 728, "Reflections on Landscape") at METU's Department of Architecture, focused on urban soil metabolism in Ankara, Turkey.

**Is this an official METU page?**
Yes — it is hosted on METU's own subdomain (`landscapelab-arch.metu.edu.tr`) as the course's dedicated web presence, curated and taught by the studio's instructors.

**Who is it for?**
Prospective and current METU students, academics interested in landscape architecture / urban soil / political ecology research, and the general public interested in Ankara's urban-environmental history.

**What kind of content is on the site?**
Eight in-depth student research projects (see above), each with research questions, position statements, methodology, field photography, maps, and drawings — plus an interactive map-based atlas for browsing them by location or theme.

**Is this open access?**
Yes — the entire studio's research output is published openly, with no paywall or login, as part of an explicit effort to make academic design research public.
